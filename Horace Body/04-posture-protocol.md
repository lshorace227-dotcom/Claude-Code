# 04 · Posture Protocol

**10 minutes a day. Every day, including rest days.**

This is the highest-return time investment in the whole project. Fat loss
takes 30 weeks to finish. Posture correction starts changing how you look
in **6 weeks** — and it changes it in exactly the places you care about:
it lifts the chest, widens the shoulder line, lengthens the neck, and
flattens the lower abdomen by resetting pelvic position.

The logic is simple: **stretch what's short, strengthen what's long.**
Both halves are required. Stretching alone gives you temporary relief and
zero lasting change.

---

## The daily 10-minute routine

Do this every morning. It's short enough that "no time" isn't a real
objection — it's shorter than scrolling your phone in bed.

### Part A — Release what's tight (4 min)

**1. Doorway pec stretch — 2 × 30 s each arm**
Forearm on the door frame, elbow at 90°, step through and rotate away.
Stretch across the front of the chest. *Targets: pectoralis major/minor —
the muscles pulling your shoulders forward.*

**2. Upper trap stretch — 30 s each side**
Sit on your hand, tilt the head away, gently assist with the opposite hand.
*Targets: upper trapezius, overworked by forward head posture.*

**3. Levator scapulae stretch — 30 s each side**
Same as above but rotate your nose toward your armpit. *Targets: the
muscle responsible for that knot at the base of your neck.*

**4. Couch stretch (hip flexor) — 60 s each side**
Rear foot elevated on a couch or chair, front foot forward in a lunge.
Squeeze the rear glute hard and tuck the pelvis under. *Targets: psoas and
rectus femoris — the primary drivers of your anterior pelvic tilt. This is
the most important stretch in Part A.*

**5. Thoracic extension over a foam roller — 60 s**
Roller across the mid-back, hands behind head, extend backward over it.
Move the roller up and down 3–4 positions. *Targets: the rounded upper
back directly.*

### Part B — Strengthen what's weak (6 min)

**6. Chin tuck — 3 × 10, 5 s hold**
Sitting or standing against a wall. Pull the chin straight back — make a
double chin — without tilting the head. Hold 5 s. *Targets: deep neck
flexors. This is the direct antidote to forward head posture.*

**7. Wall angel — 3 × 10**
Back against a wall, heels ~15 cm out, low back flat. Arms in a goal-post
shape, backs of hands touching the wall. Slide up and down while keeping
hands and forearms in contact. *If you can't keep contact, that's your
kyphosis — and this is the exercise that fixes it. Work through partial
range.*

**8. Band pull-apart — 3 × 20**
Band at chest height, arms straight, pull apart until the band touches your
chest. Squeeze shoulder blades. *Targets: rhomboids, mid-trap, rear delts.*

**9. Prone Y-raise — 3 × 10**
Face down, arms overhead in a Y, thumbs up. Lift arms off the floor using
your mid-back, not your neck. *Targets: lower trapezius — the single
weakest link in upper crossed syndrome.*

**10. Glute bridge — 3 × 15**
Feet flat, drive through the heels, squeeze the glutes hard at the top,
ribs down. *Targets: gluteus maximus. Strong glutes pull the pelvis back to
neutral and undo the tilt.*

**11. Dead bug — 3 × 8 each side**
On your back, arms up, knees at 90°. Lower opposite arm and leg slowly.
**Keep the low back flat against the floor the entire time.** *Targets:
deep core / transverse abdominis. Teaches your ribcage to stack over your
pelvis — which is what "good posture" actually is, mechanically.*

## Desk resets — every hour, 60 seconds

You spend 8+ hours a day at a screen. This is where the posture gets built,
so this is where it gets unbuilt. Set an hourly reminder.

1. **Stand up.** Non-negotiable, this is half the benefit.
2. **10 chin tucks**
3. **10 shoulder rolls backward**
4. **20 s doorway pec stretch**
5. **10 standing hip flexor pulses** each side
6. **Look at something 20 m away for 20 s** (your eyes need it too)

Sixty seconds. Twelve times a day. That's 12 minutes total and it will do
more for your neck than any single long session.

---

## Desk setup — fix the environment, not just the symptom

You can't out-stretch a bad workstation. Fix this once and it works for you
all day, every day, forever.

| Item | Correct setup |
|---|---|
| **Monitor height** | Top of screen at or slightly below eye level. If you use a laptop, you need a stand and an external keyboard. This one change is worth more than all the stretches combined. |
| **Monitor distance** | Arm's length away |
| **Chair** | Feet flat on the floor, knees ~90°, hips slightly *above* knees |
| **Lumbar support** | A cushion or rolled towel in the low back curve |
| **Elbows** | ~90°, forearms level, shoulders relaxed down |
| **Phone** | Raise it to eye level. "Text neck" is 20–25 kg of effective load on your cervical spine at a 60° tilt. |

**Standing desk:** if you can get one, alternate 30 min sitting / 30 min
standing. Standing all day is not better than sitting all day — variety is
the actual goal.

---

## The trap: don't confuse "chest up" with fixed posture

Consciously yanking your shoulders back and puffing your chest out is not
posture correction. It's a temporary muscular override that fatigues in 90
seconds and often just swaps rounded shoulders for a flared ribcage and a
*worse* pelvic tilt.

Real correction is when the neutral position becomes your default because
the underlying muscles changed. That takes the daily work. Cue yourself
with **"tall spine, ribs down, chin back"** rather than "shoulders back."

## Timeline

| Week | What to expect |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Stretches feel tight. Wall angels are humbling. Some upper-back muscle soreness — that's the lower traps waking up. |
| 3–4 | Noticeably easier. You start catching yourself slouching, which means awareness is developing. |
| **6–8** | **Visible change in side-profile photos.** Neck looks longer, chest more open. Others may comment. |
| 12–16 | Neutral position starts feeling normal rather than effortful. |
| 24–30 | Largely corrected. Ear over shoulder in a relaxed standing photo. |

---

## Monthly check

Take a side-profile photo on the 1st of every month. Same spot, same
lighting, relaxed stance, arms at your sides — **don't pose, and don't
"fix" your posture for the camera** or you'll be measuring your willpower
instead of your progress.

Then draw a vertical line down from your ear. Track where it lands:

- **Now:** the line falls well in front of your shoulder
- **Goal:** the line passes through ear → shoulder → hip → knee → ankle

That single line is the whole assessment. It's also the most satisfying
progress metric in this entire project, because unlike the scale, it only
moves in one direction.
