### What this actually says

**1. You have an athlete's engine in a sedentary man's shell.**
57.8 kg of muscle at 181 cm is above the standard range. Most 28-year-old
office workers scan *below* it. Whatever you did athletically in the past
is still on your frame. This is the asset the entire plan is built to
protect.

**2. Your fat is in the worst place, which is also the most responsive place.**
11.05 kg of 21.6 kg — 51% — sits on your trunk. Both arms combined hold
2.84 kg; both legs 7.71 kg. That's textbook male central adiposity. The
upside: visceral and central fat is metabolically active and mobilises
*first* in a calorie deficit. Your waist will move faster than the scale
in the first 8 weeks. Expect this and don't panic when weight stalls but
the belt loosens.

**3. Waist is your real scoreboard, not weight.**
95.1 cm against an 85 cm ceiling. Waist circumference predicts metabolic
risk better than BMI does, and it tracks the exact fat you want gone.
Measure it weekly.

**4. Visceral fat is borderline, not dangerous — right now.**
Level 9 and 84 cm² are under the clinical thresholds but heading the wrong
way. At 28 with a desk job, this is the fork in the road. Address it now
and it's a non-issue; ignore it for five years and it's a medical
conversation.

**5. The leg asymmetry is trivial.**
10.52 vs 10.38 kg is a 1.3% difference — inside measurement noise. The
device graded them differently only because they fall either side of a cut
line. Not worth worrying about, but the program includes unilateral leg
work anyway, which is good practice regardless.

**6. Body age 30 vs actual 28.**
Driven almost entirely by fat percentage. It falls out automatically as
fat comes off. Expect ≤ 26 at target.

## Part 2 — Postural Assessment

Read from four standing photographs (back, front, and both side views).
Visual observation only — not a clinical examination.

### Side view — the primary finding

This is where the pattern is unmistakable.

**Forward head posture.** Your ear sits clearly forward of your shoulder
line — visibly so in both side photos. Every 2.5 cm the head travels
forward roughly doubles the effective load on the neck extensors and upper
traps. This is why the base of your neck and upper shoulders probably feel
tight by evening.

**Thoracic kyphosis (rounded upper back).** Increased rounding through the
upper back, with the shoulders rolled forward and the chest slightly
collapsed. Reduces your apparent shoulder width considerably.

**Anterior pelvic tilt.** The pelvis is rotated forward, exaggerating the
lumbar curve and pushing the lower abdomen out. **This is important for
your goal:** a meaningful part of how your belly looks in the side photos
is pelvic position, not fat. Correcting the tilt flattens the lower
abdomen visually before you lose a single kilogram.

### Front view

- Shoulders rolled forward; palms face slightly backward at rest, which
  indicates internally rotated shoulders.
- Slight lateral head tilt.
- Fat distribution centrally concentrated — periumbilical and flanks,
  consistent with the 11.05 kg trunk figure.
- Chest carries some fat, which will resolve with overall fat loss.

### Back view

- Shoulders approximately level, possibly a very slight right-side drop.
- Scapulae sit protracted and lateral on the ribcage — spread apart rather
  than set down and back.
- Visible lumbar curve consistent with the anterior tilt seen from the side.

### The pattern, named

You have a textbook **Upper Crossed Syndrome** layered on a mild **Lower
Crossed Syndrome**. It's the most common postural presentation in desk
workers and it responds well to consistent corrective work.

**Upper crossed — what's tight vs. weak**

| Overactive / short (stretch these) | Underactive / long (strengthen these) |
|---|---|
| Upper trapezius | Deep neck flexors |
| Levator scapulae | Lower & middle trapezius |
| Pectoralis major & minor | Rhomboids |
| Sternocleidomastoid | Serratus anterior |
| Suboccipitals | External rotators (infraspinatus, teres minor) |

**Lower crossed — what's tight vs. weak**

| Overactive / short (stretch these) | Underactive / long (strengthen these) |
|---|---|
| Hip flexors (psoas, rectus femoris) | Glute max & medius |
| Lumbar erectors | Deep core (transverse abdominis) |
| Latissimus dorsi | Hamstrings |
| Tensor fasciae latae | Lower abdominals |

The full corrective protocol built from this table is in
[`04-posture-protocol.md`](04-posture-protocol.md).

### Prognosis

Postural patterns of this type — no structural deformity, no pain reported,
28 years old, good muscle base — typically show clear visual change in
**6–8 weeks** of daily work, and substantial correction in **4–6 months**.
That timeline lines up almost exactly with your fat loss timeline, which is
convenient: both arrive together in early 2027.

### When to see a professional

Book a physiotherapist rather than self-managing if any of these apply:

- Neck, shoulder, or low back **pain** — not tightness, actual pain
- Numbness, tingling, or weakness in the arms or hands
- Headaches originating at the base of the skull
- Pain that wakes you at night
- A visible sideways spinal curve (possible scoliosis) — photos can't rule
  this out reliably

None of these are visible in your photos, but photos can't feel anything.
You can.
