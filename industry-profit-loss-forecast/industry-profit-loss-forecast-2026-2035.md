# Industry Profit & Loss Forecast: 2026–2035

*A 10-year outlook on which industries are likely to generate the largest profits — and which are likely to destroy the most value. Prepared July 2026.*

> **Caveat:** Nobody can predict industry profits ten years out with precision. This report combines current analyst forecasts (IDC, Gartner, McKinsey, Deloitte, J.P. Morgan, Newmark) with structural reasoning about demand, capacity, and competition. Treat it as a probability-weighted map, not a guarantee. Consensus forecasts of "supercycles" have historically been wrong on timing more often than direction.

---

## Part 1: Industries likely to earn the MOST profit (2026–2035)

### 1. Semiconductors & AI infrastructure — the decade's largest profit pool
- Semiconductor revenue is forecast to reach **~$1.29 trillion in 2026** (up 52.8% YoY) and **$1.6–1.75 trillion by 2030** (IDC, McKinsey base cases).
- AI chips already account for ~30% of industry revenue; data center semiconductors are projected at **~$843 billion by 2030** — nearly half the market.
- Profit concentration is extreme: GPU/accelerator design (NVIDIA-class margins of 50%+ net), advanced foundry (TSMC), and high-bandwidth memory (DRAM revenue projected to nearly triple in 2026 to ~$419 billion).
- **Risk:** this is also the sector most exposed to a capex bust. If AI monetization lags infrastructure spend, 2028–2030 could see a brutal inventory/overcapacity correction — the classic semiconductor cycle at unprecedented scale.

### 2. Cloud hyperscalers & AI software/services
- McKinsey's "arenas of competition" work already ranks consumer internet (~$90B economic profit) and consumer electronics (~$76B) among the largest profit arenas; AI software layers on top.
- The hyperscalers (Microsoft, Amazon, Google, plus AI-native labs) capture profit twice: renting compute and selling AI products. Software gross margins (70–85%) mean any durable AI revenue converts to profit efficiently.
- Expect enterprise AI/agents, cybersecurity, and inference services to be among the highest-ROIC businesses of the decade.

### 3. Pharmaceuticals & biotech (obesity/metabolic, oncology, longevity)
- Pharma added **~$47B of economic profit growth in North America** in McKinsey's latest profit-pool analysis — second only to consumer goods.
- GLP-1/metabolic drugs are on track to be the best-selling drug class in history; oncology (ADCs, cell therapy) and aging-population demand provide a durable 10-year tailwind.
- Patent-protected pricing power makes this one of the few sectors with tech-like margins and demographics-guaranteed demand.

### 4. Electric power, grid & energy infrastructure
- AI data centers alone are projected to need **~92 GW of additional power by 2027 and up to ~176 GW by 2035** — the first sustained electricity demand growth in the developed world in decades.
- Beneficiaries: utilities with data-center exposure, gas turbine and grid-equipment makers, nuclear (including SMRs by early 2030s), and transmission builders. Regulated returns plus genuine scarcity is a rare combination.

### 5. Defense & aerospace
- Structurally elevated geopolitical tension has pushed NATO-Europe, Japan, and Gulf states into multi-year rearmament cycles with decade-long order books. Missiles, drones/counter-drone, space, and munitions capacity are supply-constrained — a recipe for sustained margin expansion.

### 6. Financials & asset management (steady compounder)
- Not a headline grower, but banking and private capital remain among the largest absolute profit pools globally. Private credit and infrastructure funds are the likely share gainers within the sector.

**Honorable mentions:** robotics (semiconductor content alone forecast to grow from $1.5B in 2026 to ~$4.9B by 2035), industrial/logistics real estate, luxury goods, and grocery-anchored retail.

## Part 2: Industries likely to suffer the MOST losses (2026–2035)

### 1. Office commercial real estate — the largest ongoing value destruction
- Office CMBS delinquencies hit a **record 12.34% in January 2026**, the worst since tracking began in 2000.
- Class B/C offices face outright obsolescence; equity in many towers is already wiped out, and losses are now migrating to lenders (regional banks, CMBS holders). Expect hundreds of billions in cumulative write-downs through ~2030 before conversion/demolition rebalances supply.

### 2. Legacy media, publishing & linear TV/cable
- BLS and IBISWorld consistently rank publishing, cable programming, and telecom among the fastest-declining industries by output. Cord-cutting plus AI-driven disruption of search/ad-funded content compresses both revenue and margins for the next decade.

### 3. Tobacco & combustible products (volume collapse, cash-cow endgame)
- Projected **~53% employment decline in tobacco manufacturing (2022–2032)**, US smoking rates at record-low ~11%. Incumbents stay cash-generative via pricing, but the industry's economic footprint shrinks relentlessly.

### 4. Legacy auto & undercapitalized EV makers
- The EV transition destroys profit on both ends: legacy OEMs burn capital retooling while Chinese competition compresses global prices, and most EV startups never reach scale. Expect continued multi-billion-dollar annual losses across the long tail of EV manufacturers and severe margin pressure on European/US mass-market OEMs through ~2030.

### 5. Thermal coal & stranded fossil assets (developed markets)
- Terminal-decline economics in OECD markets: shrinking demand, no new capital, rising closure/remediation liabilities. (Note: Asian demand keeps global volumes higher for longer than activists expect — losses concentrate in developed-market producers and their financiers.)

### 6. The AI capex losers (the decade's sleeper risk)
- The mirror image of Part 1: if $2–3 trillion of cumulative AI infrastructure spend outruns monetization, the losses land on over-leveraged data-center developers, second-tier chipmakers, and "GPU-rental" middlemen with no moat. A 2028–2031 shakeout here could rival the dot-com telecom bust in absolute dollars — even while the sector's winners stay hugely profitable.

**Also under pressure:** fast fashion and mid-tier department-store retail, traditional business-process outsourcing (AI substitution), and low-end white-collar services (entry-level legal/accounting/content work).

## Part 3: Summary table

| Rank | Biggest profit earners | Biggest loss makers |
|------|------------------------|---------------------|
| 1 | Semiconductors & AI infrastructure | Office commercial real estate |
| 2 | Cloud / AI software & services | Legacy media, publishing, linear TV |
| 3 | Pharma & biotech (GLP-1, oncology) | Tobacco / combustibles |
| 4 | Electric power & grid infrastructure | Legacy auto & subscale EV makers |
| 5 | Defense & aerospace | Thermal coal / stranded fossil assets (OECD) |
| 6 | Financials & private capital | Over-leveraged AI capex losers (late-decade risk) |

## Key swing factors that could rewrite this list
1. **AI monetization pace** — determines whether semis/cloud stay #1 or crash mid-decade.
2. **Interest rates** — higher-for-longer deepens CRE and leveraged-infrastructure losses.
3. **China/Taiwan and trade policy** — a supply shock would invert semiconductor economics overnight.
4. **Drug-pricing regulation** — the main threat to the pharma profit pool.
5. **Energy transition speed** — decides how fast fossil losses arrive and how big grid profits get.
