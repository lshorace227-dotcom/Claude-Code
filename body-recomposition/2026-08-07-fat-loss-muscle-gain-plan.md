# Body Recomposition Plan — Fat Loss + Muscle Gain

**Date:** 2026-08-07
**Profile:** Female, 28 y/o, 154 cm, 46.7 kg, moderately-high body fat %
**Goal:** Lose body fat, gain muscle ("skinny-fat" → lean and firm)
**Equipment:** Dumbbells, yoga mat, home space
**Current training:** Pilates 2×/week (Mon = abs, Sat = back/arms)

> This is general fitness and nutrition information, not medical advice. See the
> **Health Flags** section at the end — there is one item worth discussing with a doctor.

## 1. Diagnosis: why you still look soft at a low weight

BMI = 46.7 ÷ 1.54² = **19.7** — a normal, even lowish weight. At 44 kg you were BMI 18.6,
which is underweight, **and you still looked soft**. That is the whole answer:

**Your problem is not too much weight. It is not enough muscle.**

What the old protocol did:

| What you did | What it actually caused |
|---|---|
| Fasted HIIT 3–5×/week, 30 min | High cortisol / high fatigue, low muscle-building stimulus |
| Carbs under 50 g/day | Depleted glycogen (looks "flat", not firm), poor training output |
| No progressive resistance training | Nothing signalling the body to *keep* or *build* muscle |
| Aggressive calorie deficit for a long time | Weight loss came heavily from **lean mass + water**, not only fat |

When you lose muscle at roughly the same rate as fat, your **body fat percentage barely
moves**. You get smaller but the same softness — arms, stomach, thighs, back. That is
exactly what you described.

So the lever going forward is **build muscle**, not lose more weight. Expect the scale to
go **up** toward 48–50 kg over the next 6–12 months while you look visibly leaner. That is
success, not failure.

Also: **you cannot spot-reduce.** No amount of ab work removes stomach fat. Firmness comes
from muscle underneath + a modest overall fat reduction.

## 2. Nutrition

### Calorie targets

Mifflin-St Jeor BMR = (10 × 46.7) + (6.25 × 154) − (5 × 28) − 161 = **≈ 1,130 kcal**
With 2 Pilates + 3 lifting sessions + daily walking (activity factor ≈ 1.4–1.5):

**Maintenance ≈ 1,600–1,700 kcal/day**

| Phase | Duration | Intake | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Phase 1 — Recover & build** | Months 1–4 | **1,650–1,700 kcal** (maintenance) | Rebuild muscle, restore metabolism after long restriction |
| **Phase 2 — Optional mild cut** | Months 5–7, only if needed | **1,450–1,500 kcal** | Small deficit while *keeping* the new muscle |
| **Back to maintenance** | after any cut | 1,650–1,700 | Never diet continuously |

**Do not go below ~1,400 kcal.** You have already spent a long stretch under-eating; going
lower again will repeat the exact result you're unhappy with.

Starting at maintenance rather than a deficit is deliberate. Recomposition works best for
someone who is (a) new to resistance training and (b) coming off a period of under-eating —
both true for you. You will get the fat-loss look from muscle gain + slight fat loss at the
same weight.

### Macros at 1,650 kcal

| Macro | Target | Calories | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Protein** | **100–105 g** (~2.1 g/kg) | 400–420 | The single biggest lever. Non-negotiable. |
| **Fat** | **50–60 g** | 450–540 | Hormones, cycle health, satiety. Never below ~40 g. |
| **Carbs** | **170–190 g** | 680–760 | Training fuel, muscle fullness, thyroid/cycle support |
| **Fiber** | 25–30 g | — | Your veggie intake likely covers this already |

**Yes, carbs go back up — from under 50 g to ~180 g.** This is intentional. Carbs are what
let you actually push hard in a lifting session, and glycogen is what makes muscle look full
rather than flat. Some of the initial scale increase will be glycogen and water. That is not
fat.

### Gaps in your current diet

Your food list is genuinely good quality — but it is **carb-heavy relative to protein**, and
it's missing a few categories:

1. **Dairy / soy / legumes.** Add: Greek yogurt (0–2%), cottage cheese, 豆腐, 毛豆, 豆漿

**Note on 吊龍:** delicious and a fine iron/creatine source, but it's a fatty cut. Keep it to

- **Stop training fasted.** Eat 60–90 min before lifting: ~30 g carbs + ~20 g protein

- Get a protein-containing meal within ~2 hours after training. The precise window is
  overrated; hitting daily total is what matters.
- Meal frequency otherwise doesn't matter. Eat on a schedule you can sustain.

## 3. Training

### The core principle

Pilates is excellent for control, posture and core, but **it will not build noticeable muscle
on its own** — it doesn't provide progressive load. Keep both classes, and add **3 short
dumbbell strength sessions**. That's what changes how you look.

**HIIT: drop from 3–5×/week to 0–1×/week**, max 10–15 min, never fasted, never as the
main strategy. Replace that time with walking (7,000–9,000 steps/day). Walking burns nearly
as much over a week, costs almost no recovery, and doesn't compete with your lifting.

### Progressive overload — the part that actually matters

This is what your old routine was missing entirely. Doing the same workout at the same
difficulty forever produces no change.

**The rule:** the last 2 reps of every working set should be genuinely hard (1–2 reps left in
the tank). When you can complete the **top of the rep range on all sets, for 2 sessions in a
row → increase the weight** next time.

**If your dumbbells are too light to increase** (common with a home set), make each rep
harder instead, in this order:
1. **Slow the lowering phase** to 3–4 seconds
2. **Add a 1–2 second pause** at the hardest point
3. **Go unilateral** — one leg / one arm at a time
4. **Increase reps to 15–20** and add a 4th set
5. **Shorten rest** to 45 sec
6. Then: buy heavier dumbbells or an adjustable pair. Worth it — you *will* outgrow light
   ones within a few months, and that's the goal.

**Log every session.** Exercise, weight, sets, reps. A phone note is fine. Your training log
is the primary evidence that this is working — long before the mirror shows it.

## 4. Tracking progress (stop using the scale)

The scale is the worst possible metric for what you're doing, because muscle gain and fat
loss partially cancel out on it.

| Metric | Frequency | How |
|---|---|---|
| **Tape measurements** ⭐ | Every 2 weeks | Morning, fasted, before drinking. Waist at navel, hips at widest, mid-thigh, upper arm relaxed. |
| **Photos** ⭐ | Monthly | Same light, same clothes, same pose, same time of day. Front / side / back. |
| **Strength log** ⭐ | Every session | Weight × reps going up = muscle is being built. |
| Body weight | Weekly average only | Never judge a single day. Expect it to drift **up** — that's fine. |
| How clothes fit | Ongoing | Underrated and honest |

**What success looks like:** waist down 2–4 cm, arms and thighs the same or slightly *larger*,
scale up 1–2 kg, strength up substantially. That combination is exactly what you want.

### Realistic timeline

| Period | What to expect |
|---|---|
| **Weeks 1–4** | Strength jumps quickly (mostly neural). Scale up 0.5–1 kg from restored glycogen and water — **this is not fat.** Possible mild bloating as carbs return. Don't panic. |
| **Months 2–3** | Measurements start moving. Upper back and arms feel firmer. Energy and sleep noticeably better than on the old protocol. |
| **Months 4–6** | Visible recomposition. This is where photos start showing it clearly. |
| **Months 6–12** | Meaningful change to the "soft" look. Muscle gain in a trained female is roughly 0.2–0.5 kg/month at best — slow is normal, and it is permanent in a way that crash dieting never is. |

Be patient. Your last approach produced a fast scale result and no body composition change.
This one produces a slow scale result and a real body composition change.

## 5. Also matters

- **Sleep 7–9 hours.** Muscle is built during sleep. Under-sleeping raises cortisol and
  measurably impairs fat loss and recovery.
- **Steps 7,000–9,000/day.** This replaces the HIIT you're removing, at almost zero
  recovery cost.
- **Water 2–2.5 L/day.** Especially with higher protein and creatine.
- **Don't diet continuously.** Long uninterrupted restriction is how you got here.

## 6. Health flags — please read

Your previous protocol — under 50 g carbs, fasted HIIT 3–5×/week, and BMI 18.6 at 44 kg — is
a recognised risk profile for **RED-S (Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport)** / low energy
availability. It's worth taking seriously rather than dismissing.

**See a doctor if you have any of these**, currently or from that period:
- Irregular, very light, or absent menstrual cycles
- Persistently cold hands and feet
- Poor sleep, hair shedding, brittle nails
- Frequent illness, low mood, or unusual fatigue
- Any history of disordered eating patterns

Ask for bloodwork including **ferritin, vitamin D, thyroid (TSH/T3/T4), and a full blood
count.** These are cheap, standard tests and they'd tell you whether anything needs
correcting before you push training hard.

Nothing in this plan requires going lower than ~1,400 kcal. If you find yourself wanting to,
that's the signal to talk to a professional rather than cut further.
