<a name="1-diagnosis"></a>
## 1. Diagnosis — why you look soft at a low weight

BMI = 46.7 ÷ 1.54² = **19.7** — a normal, even lowish weight. At 44 kg you were BMI 18.6,
which is underweight, **and you still looked soft**. That is the whole answer:

**The problem is not too much weight. It is not enough muscle.**

What the old protocol did:

| What you did | What it actually caused |
|---|---|
| Fasted HIIT 3–5×/week, 30 min | High cortisol / high fatigue, low muscle-building stimulus |
| Carbs under 50 g/day | Depleted glycogen (looks "flat", not firm), poor training output |
| No progressive resistance training | Nothing signalling the body to *keep* or *build* muscle |
| Aggressive deficit sustained long-term | Weight loss came heavily from **lean mass + water**, not only fat |

When muscle is lost at roughly the same rate as fat, **body fat percentage barely moves**.
You get smaller with identical softness — arms, stomach, thighs, back. Exactly the outcome
you described.

So the lever going forward is **build muscle**, not lose more weight. Expect the scale to go
**up** toward 48–50 kg over 6–12 months while looking visibly leaner. That is success.

**You cannot spot-reduce.** No amount of ab work removes stomach fat. Firmness comes from
muscle underneath plus a modest overall fat reduction.

## 3. Nutrition

### Calorie targets

Mifflin-St Jeor BMR = (10 × 46.7) + (6.25 × 154) − (5 × 28) − 161 = **≈ 1,130 kcal**
With 2 Pilates + 3 lifting sessions + daily walking (activity factor ≈ 1.4–1.5):

**Maintenance ≈ 1,600–1,700 kcal/day**

| Phase | Duration | Intake | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Phase 1 — Recover & build** | Months 1–4 | **1,650–1,700 kcal** (maintenance) | Rebuild muscle, restore metabolism after long restriction |
| **Phase 2 — Optional mild cut** | Months 5–7, only if needed | **1,450–1,500 kcal** | Small deficit while *keeping* the new muscle |
| **Back to maintenance** | after any cut | 1,650–1,750 | Never diet continuously |

**Do not go below ~1,400 kcal.** You have already spent a long stretch under-eating; going
lower again repeats the exact result you're unhappy with.

Starting at maintenance rather than a deficit is deliberate — recomposition works best for
someone (a) new to resistance training and (b) coming off under-eating. Both true.

### Macros at 1,650 kcal

| Macro | Target | Calories | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Protein** | **100–105 g** (~2.1 g/kg) | 400–420 | The single biggest lever. Non-negotiable. |
| **Fat** | **50–60 g** | 450–540 | Hormones, cycle health, satiety. Never below ~40 g. |
| **Carbs** | **170–190 g** | 680–760 | Training fuel, muscle fullness, thyroid/cycle support |
| **Fiber** | 25–30 g | — | Veggie intake likely covers this already |

**Carbs go from under 50 g back up to ~180 g.** Intentional. Carbs are what let you push hard
in a lifting session, and glycogen is what makes muscle look full rather than flat. Some

Pilates is excellent for control, posture and core, but **provides no progressive load** — it
won't build noticeable muscle alone. Keep both classes, add **3 short dumbbell sessions**.

**HIIT: drop from 3–5×/week to 0–1×/week**, max 10–15 min, never fasted, never as the main
strategy. Replace with walking (7,000–9,000 steps/day) — nearly the same weekly burn, almost

---

### Progressive overload — the part that actually matters

This is what the old routine was missing entirely. Same workout at the same difficulty
forever produces no change.

**The rule:** the last 2 reps of every working set should be genuinely hard (1–2 reps in
reserve). Complete the **top of the rep range on all sets for 2 sessions running → increase
the weight.**

**If dumbbells are too light to increase**, make each rep harder instead, in this order:

1. Slow the lowering phase to 3–4 seconds
2. Add a 1–2 second pause at the hardest point
3. Go unilateral — one leg / one arm
4. Increase reps to 15–20 and add a 4th set
5. Shorten rest to 45 sec
6. Then: buy heavier dumbbells

**Log every session.** Exercise, weight, sets, reps. A phone note is fine. The training log is
the primary evidence this is working — long before the mirror shows it.

### Equipment — the real constraint

This stops most people at home, and it will hit around **week 8–14.**

Within 6 months you should be goblet squatting **16–20 kg** and RDL-ing **2×12–15 kg.** If
your dumbbells top out at 5 kg you will stall hard, and tempo tricks won't fully compensate.

**Buy adjustable dumbbells reaching at least 20 kg each** — the single highest-return purchase
for this plan. Cheaper alternative: pairs at 5 / 8 / 10 / 12.5 kg plus resistance bands.

<a name="5-why-this-works"></a>
## 5. Why this works — the mechanism

*"How can eating more not gain fat, but lose fat and gain muscle?"*

### a. "Eating more" is relative to *your* maintenance

1,650 kcal is roughly what your body burns in a day. It only feels like a lot compared to
your old restricted intake (~1,100–1,300). **Fat gain requires eating above maintenance.**
Eating *at* maintenance doesn't add fat — it just stops the deficit. You're not adding energy,
you're redirecting it.

### b. The arithmetic — the amounts involved are tiny

- **1 kg of muscle costs ~5,000–6,000 kcal to build** (muscle is ~75% water — energetically cheap)
- **1 kg of body fat stores ~7,700 kcal**

A realistic rate is ~0.3 kg muscle/month = **~1,700 kcal over a month = ~57 kcal/day.** The
body pulls that from fat stores, and 1,700 kcal of fat = **0.22 kg.**

One month at exact maintenance:

| | Change |
|---|---|
| Muscle | **+0.3 kg** |
| Fat | **−0.22 kg** |
| **Scale** | **+0.08 kg** (nothing) |
| Waist | **down** |

The two flows are small enough to cancel on the scale. Over 6 months: **+1.8 kg muscle,
−1.3 kg fat.** On a 46.7 kg frame that's a large visual change — muscle occupies ~18% less
volume per kg than fat, so you get visibly smaller at the same weight.

### c. Calories decide *how much* you weigh; protein and lifting decide *what it's made of*

Same 1,650 kcal, two outcomes:

- **Low protein, no resistance training** → nothing signals the body to build or keep muscle;
  spare energy defaults to fat.
- **100 g protein + progressive overload** → training creates the demand, protein supplies
  the material, the body preferentially routes energy into muscle repair and growth.

This is **nutrient partitioning** — the actual mechanism, and why "just eat less" produced the
result you didn't want.

### d. Why it works for *you* specifically

Recomposition isn't universal. It works best when these are true — all four are:

1. **Untrained** — never done progressive resistance training. Novices gain fastest.
2. **Coming off a long deficit** — regaining lost muscle is far faster than building new
   (muscle cells retain their nuclei).
3. **Higher fat relative to lean mass** — plenty of stored fuel to power the building.
4. **Protein was too low** — easiest variable to fix, biggest effect.

An experienced lifter at 15% body fat eating enough protein cannot do this. You can.

### e. Honest caveats

- **The scale rises in weeks 1–4 and not all of it is nothing.** 50 g → 180 g carbs adds
  ~300–500 g glycogen, each gram holding ~3 g water → 1–2 kg water weight. Creatine adds
  another 0.5–1 kg. That's most of it. But if your metabolism is currently suppressed from
  long restriction, real maintenance may sit *below* 1,650 for a few weeks, so a small amount
  of genuine fat gain is possible early. Worth it to restore metabolic rate.
- **1,650 is a formula estimate, ±10–15%.** Real maintenance is what the tape says after four
  weeks.
- **This is slow.** ~0.3 kg muscle and ~0.2 kg fat per month. It will not look dramatic at
  week 6.
- **It expires.** After 6–12 months you're no longer a novice and must pick a direction.

## 6. "Will this make me bulky?"

**Goal is the slim, defined figure — not a heavily-built influencer physique.** This plan
produces the former and cannot accidentally produce the latter.

### You cannot accidentally get there

| Period | Natural muscle gained (female) |
|---|---|
| Year 1 (best year you'll ever have) | ~2.5–3 kg |
| Year 2 | ~1–1.5 kg |
| Year 3 | ~0.5–0.7 kg |
| Year 4+ | ~0.3 kg/year |

Lifetime natural ceiling at 154 cm is maybe **5–7 kg of muscle across an entire body, over a
decade.**

The physiques in question come from 5–6 training days/week, 20+ hard sets per muscle group,
*deliberate* surpluses of 300–500 kcal held for years, heavy barbell work — and in a
meaningful share of that content, undisclosed performance-enhancing drugs.

You're doing **three 35-minute dumbbell sessions at maintenance.** Not the same activity.

### The reframe

**"Slim and toned" and "muscular" are the same road — different distances, not different
directions.** You're at kilometre zero. The look you want is at kilometre 3. The look you fear
is at kilometre 40, uphill, requiring years of deliberate surplus.

You will not blow past your exit without noticing. You'd have *years* of monthly progress
photos showing you approaching it, and stopping is as simple as no longer adding weight.
Nothing here is fast, and nothing here is irreversible.

### The uncomfortable part

The slim figure you want **is literally made of muscle.** Firm arms = deltoid and triceps
under low body fat. A flat defined stomach = abs that exist. The "lifted" look = glutes. The
line down the back = lats and rear delts.

The alternative — losing weight without building muscle — produces **skinny and soft.** You
already ran that experiment at 44 kg.

### The "toning" myth

High reps don't "tone", low reps don't "bulk". Muscle grows across essentially every rep range
when sets are taken near failure. **"Toned" just means muscle plus low body fat.** You cannot
change a muscle's shape — only its size and the fat covering it.

### What actually makes people look thick

Two things, neither in this plan:

1. **Caloric surplus** — muscle *plus* a fat layer reads as thick. Physique athletes look big
   in the off-season and defined when they cut: same muscle, different fat. **You're at
   maintenance** — definition without the layer.
2. **Deliberate specialization** — heavy shrugs (thick neck), very high-volume quad work
   (thick legs), 20+ glute sets/week. This plan has **six** glute-focused sets a week and no
   shrugs at all.

### The muscles that make you look *smaller*

| Muscle | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Lateral delts** ⭐ | Widens shoulders slightly → waist looks **smaller by contrast.** The most "slimming" muscle on the body. |
| **Upper back / rear delts** ⭐ | V-taper, plus posture — standing tall alone takes visual centimetres off a stomach |
| **Glutes + hamstrings** | Shape and leg line without adding thigh circumference the way quad specialization does |
| **Triceps** | The back-of-arm softness |

Day B's last exercises are lateral raises and reverse flies. **The program was written for the
slim look from the start.**

### Two optional tweaks to lean further in

- **Keep quad volume where it is** — don't add squat/lunge work beyond what's written.
- **Never skip lateral raises and reverse flies.**

If anything ever looks bigger than you want, drop that exercise and it reverses in weeks. At
0.3 kg of muscle per month across the whole body, that decision is years away — and you'll
have the photos to see it coming.

## 7. Tracking progress

The scale is the worst possible metric here, because muscle gain and fat loss cancel on it.

| Metric | Frequency | How |
|---|---|---|
| **Tape measurements** ⭐ | Every 2 weeks | Morning, fasted, before drinking. Waist at navel, hips at widest, mid-thigh, upper arm relaxed. |
| **Photos** ⭐ | Monthly | Protocol below |
| **Strength log** ⭐ | Every session | Weight × reps rising = muscle being built |
| Body weight | Weekly average only | Never judge a single day. Expect it to drift **up.** |
| How clothes fit | Ongoing | Underrated and honest |

**Success looks like:** waist down 2–4 cm, arms and thighs the same or slightly *larger*,
scale up 1–2 kg, strength up substantially.

### Photo protocol ⭐ NEW

The 2026-08-08 baseline set works, but standardize from here so comparisons are honest:

- **Morning, fasted, after the bathroom, before drinking** — evening abdominal distension is
  real and will make you think you regressed
- **Natural light from the front**, not overhead warm lighting — an overhead pendant casts
  downward shadows that exaggerate everything
- **Same spot, same clothes, same distance, feet marked** on the floor
- **Relaxed and neutral** — don't suck in, don't pose. Then take one flexed shot, it's
  genuinely informative
- **Same week of your cycle** each month

Keep the flexed back shot. In six months it will be the most satisfying comparison you have.

### Realistic timeline

| Period | What to expect |
|---|---|
| **Weeks 1–4** | Strength jumps quickly (mostly neural). Scale up 0.5–1 kg from restored glycogen and water — **not fat.** Possible mild bloating as carbs return. |
| **Months 2–3** | Measurements move. **Posture change becomes visible in the side profile.** Upper back and arms feel firmer. Energy and sleep better than on the old protocol. |
| **Months 4–6** | Visible recomposition. Photos start showing it clearly. |
| **Months 6–12** | Meaningful change to the soft look. ~0.2–0.5 kg muscle/month at best — slow is normal, and permanent in a way crash dieting never is. |

The last approach produced a fast scale result and no composition change. This one produces a
slow scale result and a real composition change.

## 8. The week-8 checkpoint

Check tape measurements against the training log. **One adjustment maximum**, then keep going.

| Result | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Waist flat or down + strength clearly up | Working | **Change nothing** |
| Waist up 2+ cm + strength up | Real surplus | Drop 150 kcal (carbs), re-check in 4 weeks |
| Waist up + strength flat | Overeating and under-training | Fix the training log first, not the food |
| Waist down + strength flat/dropping | Under-eating | Add 150 kcal |

Week 8 is a checkpoint, **not a phase end.** The plan continues either way.

<a name="9-roadmap-beyond-week-8"></a>
## 9. Roadmap beyond week 8

**The program doesn't expire — progress does.** Keep the same plan as long as the weights in
your log are climbing. Change it when they stop, not on a calendar date.

### Weeks 9–12 — same plan, heavier

Nothing changes but the numbers. Realistic by now: goblet squat ~6 kg → ~10–12 kg, RDL 2×5 kg
→ 2×9–10 kg. If those haven't moved, *that's* the problem — not exercise selection.

**Week 12: deload week.** Same exercises, same weights, **half the sets.** One week. Skip it
and you'll stall around week 14 wondering why.

### Weeks 13–24 — same structure, refreshed exercises

Swap movements but keep the *pattern* identical (a squat stays a squat, a hinge stays a hinge):

| Old | New |
|---|---|
| Goblet squat | DB front squat (both DBs at shoulders) |
| DB reverse lunge | Walking lunge or lateral lunge |
| DB floor press | Push-up variations / single-arm floor press |
| One-arm row | Chest-supported row or wide-grip row |
| Shoulder press | Arnold press or Z-press |

Also increase from 3 → **4 sets** on the first two exercises of each day. Volume is the main
progression lever once weight increases slow.

**Nutrition:** unchanged at 1,650, or nudge to 1,700–1,750 if strength is consistently rising
and the tape is stable. More muscle = higher maintenance. Intake should slowly *rise* over
time, not fall.

**Week 24: deload again.**

### Month 6 — the fork

| Option | When | Protocol |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Keep recomping** ⭐ *most likely correct* | Waist down, strength up, looking better | Change nothing. Novice recomposition often runs a full year. |
| **B. Short cut** | Visible muscle built, still want less fat | 1,450–1,500 kcal for **8–12 weeks max.** Protein stays 100 g, lifting stays identical — do not reduce weights, do not add cardio. Then back to maintenance. |
| **C. Small surplus** | Frustrated by slow muscle gain | 1,800–1,850 kcal for 12–16 weeks. Builds muscle fastest; arguably the most honest answer to "I look soft" — but the scale climbs and most people can't tolerate it psychologically. |

**The rule that matters most: don't cut until you have something worth revealing.** A cut
*reveals* muscle, it doesn't create it. Cutting at month 3 puts you back at 44 kg and soft —
the exact loop already run.

### When to actually change the program

When **any one** of these is true — not on a schedule:

- Same weight × same reps for **3 consecutive sessions** on a lift, with decent sleep and food
- Dumbbells maxed out on the main lifts
- Bored enough to be skipping sessions (adherence beats optimization)

Otherwise: same plan, heavier weights, indefinitely. Boring consistency is the whole method.

**Measure every 2 weeks, change something every 4 weeks minimum.** Two-week data is mostly
noise — water, cycle phase, sodium, digestion.

<a name="10-health-flags"></a>
## 10. Health flags — please read

The previous protocol — under 50 g carbs, fasted HIIT 3–5×/week, BMI 18.6 at 44 kg — is a
recognised risk profile for **RED-S (Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport)** / low energy
availability. Worth taking seriously rather than dismissing.

**See a doctor if any of these apply**, currently or from that period:

- Irregular, very light, or absent menstrual cycles
- Persistently cold hands and feet
- Poor sleep, hair shedding, brittle nails
- Frequent illness, low mood, or unusual fatigue
- Any history of disordered eating patterns

Ask for bloodwork including **ferritin, vitamin D, thyroid (TSH/T3/T4), and a full blood
count.** Cheap standard tests that would show whether anything needs correcting before
training hard.

Nothing in this plan requires going below ~1,400 kcal. Wanting to is the signal to talk to a
professional rather than cut further.

## 11. Quick summary

1. **Eat more, not less** — 1,650–1,700 kcal at maintenance for at least 4 months.
2. **100 g protein daily**, spread over 3–4 meals. Biggest single lever.
3. **Carbs back to ~180 g.** The existing carb sources are already good.
4. **3 dumbbell sessions/week** with progressive overload. Keep both Pilates classes.
5. **HIIT down to 0–1×/week**, never fasted. Walk 7–9k steps instead.
6. **5-minute posture block 4×/week** — fastest visible win, addresses the lower belly.
7. **Hip thrusts first on Tuesday. Never skip lateral raises or reverse flies.**
8. **Track tape, photos, and strength — not the scale.** Expect the scale to rise.
9. **You will not get bulky.** Physically cannot happen at this rate, at maintenance.
10. **Give it 6 months.** Consider creatine. Get bloodwork if any health flags apply.
